Lease vs buy an EV in India: a practical framework

The monthly figure is only one input. Holding period, residual risk, real mileage and charging access usually matter more.

People often compare a lease EMI with a loan EMI and stop there. That misses the main differences between leasing and owning an electric vehicle in India.

1. How long will you keep the vehicle?

If you expect to change vehicles every two to four years, leasing can reduce the hassle of resale and transfers residual value risk to the lessor. If you plan to keep the vehicle for six years or more, ownership often works out cheaper in total cost of ownership, assuming the vehicle remains reliable and battery health is acceptable.

2. Who carries residual value risk?

Electric vehicles can depreciate faster or more unpredictably than mature ICE models, especially when new generations launch with better range or lower prices. In a lease, that risk sits with the lessor (and is priced into the monthly payment). In ownership, it sits with you.

Rule of thumb: If you are uncomfortable forecasting resale value three years out, leasing is a way to pay someone else to take that uncertainty — at a cost.

3. What is your real annual mileage?

High annual kilometres make the lower energy cost of EVs more valuable. Low annual kilometres shrink that advantage and make fixed costs (depreciation or lease margin, insurance, parking) relatively larger. Use a realistic daily or monthly km figure, not a best-case commute.

4. Can you charge at home or at work?

Home or workplace charging is still the biggest practical advantage for most private users. Relying mainly on public DC fast charging raises cost per kilometre and adds time friction. If you cannot charge overnight most days, model that explicitly before choosing either path.

5. Tax and accounting (especially for businesses)

For companies, lease payments may be treated differently from depreciation and interest on a purchased asset. The right answer depends on tax position and accounting policy. This site does not provide tax advice; treat this as a prompt to check with a qualified advisor.

Putting it together

  • Short horizon + dislike of residual risk → lean lease
  • Long horizon + ability to charge at home → lean buy
  • High mileage → EV energy advantage becomes more important either way
  • Always compare total cost over your actual holding period, not only the monthly number
