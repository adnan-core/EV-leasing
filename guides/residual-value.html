Residual value risk on electric vehicles

Resale value is one of the largest unknowns in EV ownership. Leasing shifts that uncertainty to the lessor — and prices it into the monthly payment.

Why residuals can be harder to predict

New EV models often arrive with better range, faster charging or lower list prices. That can pressure the used value of earlier models. Battery health, warranty remaining and software support also affect buyer confidence in the second-hand market.

What this means for owners

If you buy, you hold the residual risk. A steeper drop in used prices than you expected increases your true cost of ownership. If you lease, the lessor holds that risk; you pay for the transfer through the lease rate and any excess-wear or excess-km charges.

Residual risk is not automatically “good” or “bad”. It is a trade: certainty versus potential upside (or downside) on resale.

Questions worth asking

  • How long do you plan to keep the vehicle?
  • How comfortable are you estimating used value three or four years out?
  • Does the lease contract define excess kilometres and wear clearly?
  • What battery warranty remains at the end of your expected holding period if you buy?
