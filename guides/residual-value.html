Residual value risk on electric vehicles

Battery technology, price changes and buyer confidence can move used-EV values quickly. That uncertainty matters whether you lease or own.

Residual value is what a vehicle may be worth at the end of your holding period. It is one of the largest variables in total cost of ownership because the purchase price is paid once but the value loss is spread across every year you keep the vehicle.

Why EV values can move faster

New models may arrive with more range, faster charging or lower prices. Incentive changes, battery-health questions and a still-developing used-EV market can make resale outcomes harder to predict than for mature petrol segments.

Leasing transfers, but does not erase, the risk

With a lease, the lessor usually prices expected depreciation into the monthly payment and takes responsibility for resale at the end. You still pay for that risk, but the exposure is easier to budget. With ownership, you keep the upside and carry the downside directly.

How to compare responsibly

  • Use a conservative end-of-term value rather than an optimistic resale quote.
  • Compare the same tenure and annual mileage across lease and buy scenarios.
  • Check whether battery warranty, buyout terms and excess-wear rules are included.
  • Run a short-horizon and long-horizon case before deciding.

Decision prompt: If avoiding resale uncertainty is valuable to you, compare the lease premium against the flexibility and predictability it provides.
