What actually drives EV running costs

Energy is only part of the story. Maintenance, tyres and where you charge often matter as much as the per-unit electricity price.

Energy cost

Home or office AC charging is usually the cheapest. Public DC fast charging is convenient but more expensive per kWh. Your mix of home vs public charging changes the real cost per kilometre more than the brochure efficiency number alone.

Maintenance

EVs typically have fewer wear items than ICE vehicles (no engine oil changes, fewer exhaust-related parts). Brakes may last longer because of regenerative braking. You still pay for tyres, cabin filters, brake fluid and scheduled checks. Battery health and warranty terms affect long-term cost risk.

Tyres and weight

Many EVs are heavier than comparable ICE models. That can mean faster tyre wear and higher replacement cost. Aggressive driving and poor road surfaces amplify this.

Insurance and fixed costs

Insurance premiums, parking and any subscription or software fees are independent of how many kilometres you drive. At low annual mileage, fixed costs dominate the total picture.
