What actually drives EV running costs

The cheapest kilometre is not determined by the battery alone. Your tariff, charging routine, mileage and service pattern shape the total.

Electric vehicles usually have fewer moving parts than petrol cars, but their running costs still vary widely. A useful estimate separates energy from maintenance and makes the assumptions visible.

Energy is the first line item

Start with usable efficiency in kWh per 100 km, then multiply by your home, workplace or public charging tariff. Charging losses add a small margin, so a wall-to-wheel estimate is more useful than the headline battery capacity.

Charging access changes the answer

Home charging is generally easier to model and can be cheaper than repeated public fast charging. If you cannot charge overnight or at work, include the extra time, access fees and higher tariff in your comparison.

Maintenance is lower, not zero

There is no engine oil or exhaust system, but tyres, brakes, suspension, cabin filters, software and periodic inspections still matter. Heavy vehicles and high torque can also change tyre-wear assumptions.

Practical shortcut: Put annual kilometres, real-world efficiency, blended charging tariff and a maintenance reserve into the running-cost calculator before comparing monthly lease figures.
